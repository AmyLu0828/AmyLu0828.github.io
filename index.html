Hello, I’m Amy.

I build and study reliable, useful AI systems.

I’m a computer science student at Stanford University interested in reliable agentic systems, multi-agent workflows, machine learning, and the infrastructure that turns research ideas into dependable products.

I currently conduct research with Stanford Trustworthy AI Research under Professor Sanmi Koyejo, working on the reliability and evaluation of agentic systems. I also enjoy building end-to-end tools across AI, backend systems, and product experiences.

My research asks how we can make AI agents more dependable: how to verify what they produce, understand and manage their failures. My newest work, Sentry, is a self-evolving runtime failure management layer for agents. It looks over agent trajectories at test-time, intervenes when failures arise, verifies whether its repairs succeed, and learns from successful recoveries. We are also developing an agentic benchmark for autoformalization to evaluate how effectively agents can translate production level source code into formal, machine-checkable outputs.

Beyond research, I’m interested in building systems that reduce friction in everyday workflows. I’ve built an agent memory layer that helps creators improve their social media performance, a procurement platform for sealed-bid auctions, and automated video-editing tools. I especially enjoy projects that require thoughtful system design.

I also love choreography and urban dance, and I’m part of Alliance, a Stanford dance team. Dance is one of the ways I express myself, explore new ideas, and connect with others.

Before Stanford, I attended Stanford Online High School, where I focused on mathematics. I explored automated geometry theorem proving at the Beijing Institute for General AI and was named a National Finalist in the S.-T. Yau High School Science Award. I also attended the Stanford University Mathematics Camp, where I studied algebraic topology and abstract algebra. These experiences continue to shape how I think about reasoning, structure, and intelligent systems.

Hello, I’m Amy.

I build and study reliable, useful AI systems.

I’m a computer science student at Stanford University interested in reliable agentic systems, multi-agent workflows, machine learning, and the infrastructure that turns research ideas into dependable products.

I currently conduct research with Stanford Trustworthy AI Research under Professor Sanmi Koyejo, working on the reliability and evaluation of agentic systems. I also enjoy building end-to-end tools across AI, backend systems, and product experiences.

My research asks how we can make AI agents more dependable: how to verify what they produce, understand and manage their failures, and evaluate whether model behaviors have truly been removed. I’m currently contributing to work on program verification, self-evolving failure management for agentic LLM systems, and shortcut restoration after machine unlearning.

Beyond research, I’m interested in the full path from an idea to a working system. I’ve built agent memory and safety infrastructure, conversational product experiences, multi-agent development tools, and applications spanning video editing, campus recommendations, and résumé generation. I especially enjoy projects that combine careful system design with practical machine learning.

Outside of computer science, I love choreography and urban dance, and I’m part of a Stanford dance team. Dance is one of the ways I express myself, explore new ideas, and connect with others.

Before Stanford, I attended Stanford Online High School, where I focused on mathematics. I explored automated geometry theorem proving at the Beijing Institute for General AI and was named a National Finalist in the S.-T. Yau High School Science Award. I also attended the Stanford University Mathematics Camp, where I studied algebraic topology, abstract algebra, and symmetry in Hopfield networks. These experiences continue to shape how I think about reasoning, structure, and intelligent systems.
